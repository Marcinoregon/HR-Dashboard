Part 1 — Wage Equity Bivariate Check

Testing for Gaps in Compensation

An initial audit reveals disparities in manager wages. Denise wants to know if these gaps are statistically significant or just random noise. We start with bivariate checks using the **T-Test** tool.

Discussion Question 1
Run a T-test comparing manager wages by **Race (White vs URM)**. What is the average wage for White managers? What is the average wage for URM managers? What is the t-statistic? Is this gap statistically significant?
Dashboard: T-Test → Grouping: Race → Metric: Manager Wage ($)
Discussion Question 2
Run a T-test comparing manager wages by **Gender (Male vs Female)**. What is the average wage for Male managers? What is the average wage for Female managers? What is the t-statistic? Is this gap statistically significant?
Dashboard: T-Test → Grouping: Gender → Metric: Manager Wage ($)
Discussion Question 3
If we stop our analysis here, what conclusions might a regulator draw about gender equity vs. racial equity at Mr. Macky's Cajun Cuisine? Explain why bivariate comparisons (T-tests) are insufficient for proving pay discrimination.
Think about: Confounding variables (e.g. experience, performance, market size) that are omitted in a simple T-test.
Part 2 — OLS Regression: The Wage Structure

OLS Regression: Controlling for Key Drivers

To see if pay disparities persist after controlling for legitimate business reasons (experience, performance, market cost of living), we run a multivariate **OLS Regression**.

🛠️ OLS Configuration for Wage Model:

  • Go to the OLS Regression tab in the sidebar.
  • Select Dataset: Manager-Level (N=145)
  • Dependent Variable (Y): Annual Wage ($)
  • Check Independent Variables (IVs / X): Experience (yrs), Performance Rating, Gender, Race/Ethnicity, Market Type
  • Click Run OLS Regression.
Discussion Question 4
Report the model's fit. What is the R-squared value? What percentage of the variance in manager wages is explained by your regression model?
Look at the top table: R-Squared.
Discussion Question 5
What is the coefficient for **Experience (yrs)**? What does this mean? Does gaining another year of prior experience increase or decrease a manager's salary, and by how much? Explain this paradox.
Look at the coefficient for exp. Why would experienced managers be paid *less* at Mr. Macky's? Recall the "Background" narrative about expansion history.
Discussion Question 6
What is the coefficient for **Performance Rating**? Is it statistically significant? If an employee raises their performance rating by 1.0 point, how much does their expected wage change?
Check the coefficient and p-value for perf.
Discussion Question 7
Examine the demographic coefficients for **Gender (Male vs Female)** and **Race/Ethnicity (White vs URM)**. After controlling for experience, performance, and market size, does a wage gap exist for Female or URM managers? How do these coefficients compare to the simple differences in averages you found in Part 1?
Dummies: gender (Male vs Female Reference) and race (White vs URM Reference). Note that in OLS, nominal reference categories represent the baseline.
Discussion Question 8
Explain the coefficient for **Market Type (Large vs Medium)**. What is the premium for working in a Large Market? Does this premium appear to reflect cost-of-living differences or economic differences between the units?
Look at the coefficient for market (Large vs Medium Reference).
Part 3 — OLS Regression: The Profitability Drivers

Business Case for HR: Modeling Net Profit

Denise needs to convince the board that employee retention and engagement are not just "nice to have," but have a direct financial impact on the bottom line. We analyze this at the restaurant-unit level.

🛠️ OLS Configuration for Profit Model:

  • Select Dataset: Restaurant-Unit Level (N=148)
  • Dependent Variable (Y): Net Profit ($)
  • Check Independent Variables (IVs / X): Turnover Rate (%), Avg Engagement Score, Payroll Cost ($), Market Type
  • Click Run OLS Regression.
Discussion Question 9
What is the coefficient for **Avg Engagement Score**? Is it statistically significant? If a manager improves their unit's average engagement score by 1.0 point (e.g. from 4.0 to 5.0), what is the estimated impact on that restaurant's net profit?
Look at the coefficient and p-value for avgEngagement.
Discussion Question 10
What is the coefficient for **Turnover Rate (%)**? Is it positive or negative? Explain why this coefficient value is highly counterintuitive. Propose a statistical or operational reason (e.g. omitted variable bias, market type interaction, or causal direction) for this paradox.
Look at the coefficient for turnoverRate. Why might units with higher turnover rates also show higher profits in this linear model?
Discussion Question 11
What is the coefficient for **Payroll Cost ($)**? What does this coefficient say about the cost structure? Does a $1.00 increase in payroll reduce profit by exactly $1.00, or by less? What does this imply about the relationship between higher wages, productivity, and sales?
Look at the coefficient for payroll. (Hint: if it's -0.35, then a dollar increase in payroll only reduces profit by 35 cents because it is offset by increased sales or productivity!).
Discussion Question 12
Using the **Explore Data** tool, filter the dataset to find the 3 units with the highest `Turnover Rate (%)`. Report their unit IDs, turnover rates, and net profits. Now find the 3 units with the lowest turnover rates and report the same. Does this simple comparison support or challenge the positive turnover coefficient in the regression?
Dashboard: Explore Data → search/sort by Turnover Rate.
Part 4 — Logistic Regression: Hiring Selection Bias

Modeling Selection Decisions

The EEOC complaint centers on the hiring selection process. To determine if selection bias exists after controlling for qualifications, we use the multivariate **Logistic Regression** tool.

🛠️ Logistic Regression Configuration:

  • Go to the Logistic Regression tab in the sidebar.
  • Check Independent Variables (IVs): **Race (White vs URM)**, **Gender (Male vs Female)**, **Prior Experience (yrs)**, **First Interview Score**
  • Click Run Analysis.
Discussion Question 13
What is the Odds Ratio for **First Interview Score**? Is it statistically significant? What does this tell you about the predictive power of the first interview in the hiring process?
Look at the Odds Ratio (OR) and p-value for First Interview Score.
Discussion Question 14
What is the Odds Ratio for **Race (White vs URM)**? Is it statistically significant? What does this ratio imply about the hiring chances of a White applicant compared to a URM applicant with identical interview scores and prior experience?
Look at the OR for Race: White (vs URM). An OR > 1 means White candidates are more likely to be selected.
Discussion Question 15
What is the Odds Ratio for **Gender (Male vs Female)**? Is it statistically significant? What does this suggest about gender equity in the selection process?
Look at the OR for Gender: Male (vs Female).
Final Deliverable

The Board Memo: Designing the Business Case

Synthesize your findings from Parts 1–4 into a formal executive memo to Denise Macky and the Board of Directors. Support your recommendations with exact statistical outputs (R2, coefficients, p-values, odds ratios).

📝 Your memo should address:

  1. Manager Wage Equity — Is there pay discrimination at Mr. Macky's? Contrast your bivariate T-test findings with the OLS Regression results. Address why Male managers earn more and what controls reveal about URM manager pay.
  2. The Experience-Wage Dilemma — Legally and operationally, how should the company address the negative coefficient on prior experience? Suggest a revised pay philosophy.
  3. EEOC Hiring Exposure — What does the Logistic Regression prove about bias in the interview and selection process? How should Denise defend or alter hiring practices?
  4. Business Case for Engagement — Use the unit profit OLS coefficients to calculate the expected financial return of a 0.5-point increase in average employee engagement. Compare this to the proposed $1.2 million budget.
  5. Confounding Variables & Cautions — Advise the board on the turnover-profit paradox. Warn them about correlation vs. causation and explain why simply increasing turnover is not a path to profitability.
